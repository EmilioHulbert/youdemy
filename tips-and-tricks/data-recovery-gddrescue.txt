### What Causes "Bad Drives"

Hard drives and SSDs are complex electromechanical or electronic devices. Over time, they degrade due to several factors:

* **Physical Surface Degradation (HDDs):** Hard drives use a magnetic coating on spinning platters. Over time, microscopic particles, head crashes (where the read-write head touches the platter), or general wear can cause "bad sectors"—areas where the magnetic charge can no longer be reliably read or written.
* **Flash Cell Wear (SSDs/USB Drives):** Flash memory uses electrical charges stored in cells. These cells have a limited number of "write cycles." Once a cell wears out, it can no longer reliably hold a charge, leading to read errors.
* **Firmware/Controller Failure:** The controller manages the mapping between logical addresses (what the OS sees) and physical locations. If the controller's internal mapping table gets corrupted or the controller chip itself begins to fail, you get intermittent communication errors (like the `uas_eh_abort_handler` you saw earlier).
* **Environmental/Mechanical Stress:** Heat, sudden power loss while writing, and physical impact can all accelerate the failure of the sensitive internal components.

VBoxManage showhdinfo /mnt/Windows10-Enterprise.vdi

### 2. Why did Mega cut its 50GB quota?

The move from 50GB to 20GB (and the tightening of their free tier) was purely economic.

* **The "Freemium" trap:** When a company is small, they give away huge storage to gain market share. Once they have millions of users, the cost of electricity, cooling, and hardware maintenance for that "free" data becomes astronomical.
* **Encrypted overhead:** Because Mega uses end-to-end encryption, they can't de-duplicate data across users (e.g., if 100 people upload the same movie file, they have to store it 100 times because they can't "see" it to de-duplicate). That makes their storage costs per user much higher than Google's.
* **The pivot to enterprise:** They shifted focus to paying professional users who need privacy/security, and the free users became a cost center they needed to shrink.
