That `VERR_PDM_NO_USB_PORTS` error combined with `NS_ERROR_FAILURE` is a massive clue. It tells us exactly why your flash drives work but your external hard drive (the Seagate ST500LT0) throws a tantrum.

Here is the breakdown of why this is happening and how to fix it on Parrot OS.

---

### Why is this happening?

1. **USB 3.0 vs. USB 2.0:** Your flash drives are likely falling back to USB 2.0 speeds or your VM is configured with a basic USB 1.1/2.0 controller. External hard drives like your Seagate require a **USB 3.0 (xHCI)** controller to draw enough power and handle the protocol handshake properly. If the VM's virtual controller doesn't have an available USB 3.0 port configured, VirtualBox throws `VERR_PDM_NO_USB_PORTS`.
2. **The Oracle Extension Pack Missing:** By default, open-source VirtualBox (the version usually found in Debian/Parrot repositories) only supports USB 1.1 out of the box. To get USB 2.0 and USB 3.0 pass-through working seamlessly for power-heavy devices like hard drives, you **must** have the matching VirtualBox Extension Pack installed on your host.
3. **Host Permission / Group Filter Issues:** Parrot OS might be locking down the raw USB interface for a spinning hard disk compared to a simple flash drive because of strict user permissions (`vboxusers` group).

---

### Step-by-Step Fix

Follow these steps in order to clear this error up.

#### Step 1: Install the VirtualBox Extension Pack on Parrot OS

Since you are on Parrot OS Home, open your terminal and check your VirtualBox version first, then grab the extension pack.

1. Check your installed version:
```bash
vboxmanage --version

```


2. Download and install the **matching version** of the Extension Pack from the official VirtualBox website, or use `apt` if it's available in your repository:
```bash
sudo apt update
sudo apt install virtualbox-ext-pack

```


*(Accept the license agreement when prompted).*

#### Step 2: Change the VM's USB Controller Settings

This is where the `VERR_PDM_NO_USB_PORTS` error lives.

1. **Shut down** your Windows 10 Enterprise VM completely (don't just save the state).
2. Open the main VirtualBox manager window.
3. Select your Windows 10 VM and click **Settings** (or press `Ctrl + S`).
4. Go to the **USB** section on the left panel.
5. Ensure **Enable USB Controller** is checked.
6. Switch the radio button from *USB 1.1 (OHCI)* or *USB 2.0 (EHCI)* to **USB 3.0 (xHCI) Controller**.

> **Note:** If USB 3.0 is greyed out, it confirms you are missing the Extension Pack from Step 1.

#### Step 3: Ensure your User is in the `vboxusers` Group

Linux hosts require your user account to have explicit permissions to pass raw hardware access over to a guest machine.

1. Open your terminal on Parrot OS and run:
```bash
sudo usermod -aG vboxusers $USER

```


2. **Crucial:** You must **log out of Parrot OS and log back in** (or restart your PC) for this group change to take effect.

#### Step 4: Add a Specific USB Filter

Instead of attaching it on the fly via the menu while the VM is running (which can cause timing issues with host OS file managers grabbing the drive first), create a dedicated filter.

1. With the drive plugged into your PC, go back to the VM's **Settings** > **USB**.
2. Click the blue **"Plus (+)" icon** on the right side of the screen.
3. Select your **ST500LT0 [0156]** drive from the list. This adds it as a permanent filter.
4. Boot up your Windows 10 VM. VirtualBox will now actively intercept the hard drive the moment the VM boots up before Parrot OS can lock it down.

Give that a shot—switching to the **USB 3.0 (xHCI)** controller is usually the silver bullet for this specific error code!
