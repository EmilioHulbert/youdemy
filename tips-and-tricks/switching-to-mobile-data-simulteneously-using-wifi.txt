That classic Android "smart network" trap. Your phone sees the Wi-Fi connection, latches onto it, and assumes that's where the internet should come from—completely ignoring your mobile data even though the Wi-Fi has no actual internet backbone.

Because Android handles ADB/Wireless Debugging over the local network (Wi-Fi), we **need** that Wi-Fi connection alive, but we also need your phone to use mobile data for its internet.

Here is how you can force your Oppo A18 (ColorOS) to use both simultaneously, or bypass this roadblock entirely.

---

### Solution 1: Enable "Mobile Data Always Active" (The Best Fix)

By default, Android shuts off the mobile data modem the second you connect to Wi-Fi. You can force Android to keep both active, allowing it to fall back to mobile data for internet while keeping the Wi-Fi local network alive for `scrcpy`.

1. Go to **Settings** > **About Device** > **Version**.
2. Tap **Version Number** (or Build Number) **7 times** until it says "You are now a developer."
3. Go back to the main **Settings** menu > **Additional Settings** > **Developer Options**.
4. Scroll down to the **Networking** section.
5. Toggle **ON** **"Mobile data always active"** (sometimes called *Always keep mobile data active*).

> **Why this works:** This forces the phone to route internet traffic through your cellular network while keeping the local Wi-Fi connection intact for your laptop to talk to the phone via wireless ADB.

### Solution 2: Turn off "Smart Wi-Fi" / Wi-Fi Assistant

ColorOS has an aggressive feature that tries to manage poor internet connections, which sometimes breaks local-only networks.

1. Go to **Settings** > **Wi-Fi**.
2. Look for **Wi-Fi Assistant** (or tap the three dots/gear icon in the top right).
3. **Disable** **"Auto-connect to the best Wi-Fi network"** or **"Auto-switch to mobile network"**.
*Note: If this is enabled, the phone might constantly drop the laptop's hotspot because it realizes there's no internet, which kills your wireless debugging session.*

### Solution 3: Reverse the Setup (Hotspot from Phone instead)

If your laptop's hotspot configuration is still giving you routing headaches, reverse the roles. It achieves the exact same local network link for `scrcpy`.

1. Turn on the **Personal Hotspot** on your **Oppo A18**. (This keeps your phone's mobile data working perfectly).
2. Connect your **laptop** to your phone’s Wi-Fi hotspot.
3. Your phone and laptop are now on the same local network.
4. Go to Developer Options on the phone, enable **Wireless Debugging**, and pair it with `scrcpy` using the IP address assigned by the phone's hotspot.

---

### Quick `scrcpy` Tip Once Connected

Once the network issue is sorted and you run `adb tcpip 5555`, you can connect over the network using:

```bash
scrcpy -e

```

*(The `-e` flag tells scrcpy to explicitly look for a TCP/IP / ethernet connection).* Try Solution 1 first—it’s usually the instant fix for this exact setup! Let me know if it gives you any trouble.
