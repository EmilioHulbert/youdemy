Using that `rsync` backup is a lifesaver. Since your `/home`, `/root`, and `/mnt` are fine, we are going to perform a "surgical restore." We will copy back the system binaries (`/usr`, `/bin`, `/sbin`, `/lib`) and the configuration files (`/etc`) from the backup drive to your live system.

Since you are currently logged into the broken desktop, we need to be careful. Here is how to restore the system files without wiping your personal data.

### 1. Identify the Backup Path

Ensure your drive is mounted at `/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/`. Run `ls /media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/` to make sure you see the `usr`, `etc`, and `var` folders there.

### 2. The Surgical Restore Command

We will use `rsync` to pull the missing files back. **Note:** We are specifically avoiding `/home`, `/root`, and `/mnt` as per your request.

Run this as root:

```bash
rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/etc/ /etc/

rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/usr/ /usr/

rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/var/ /var/

rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/bin/ /bin/

rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/sbin/ /sbin/

rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/lib/ /lib/

rsync -avPh --info=progress2 \
/media/hulbert/LINUX_DATA/lib64/ /lib64/

```

### 3. Why this works

* **`/etc/`**: Restores your package sources and desktop configurations.
* **`/usr/`**: This is where `caja`, the MATE desktop environment, and all your apps live.
* **`/var/`**: Restores the `apt` database so the system remembers what is installed.
* **`/bin/`, `/sbin/`, `/lib/**`: Restores essential system commands and drivers.

You’re absolutely right: backups are the ultimate insurance policy. In the Linux world, we have a few "commandments" about this. Here are some of the best phrases to live by:

### The Golden Rules of Backups

* **"Two is one, and one is none."**
* If you only have one copy of your data, you don't actually have a copy—you just have a single point of failure.


* **"A backup is not a backup until you have restored from it."**
* You just proved yours works! Many people realize too late that their backup files were corrupted or incomplete.


* **"There are two types of people: those who back up, and those who haven't lost everything yet."**
* You officially graduated to the first group today.


* **"Jesus saves, but incremental backups save often."**
* A classic sysadmin joke. Using `rsync` for incremental updates is the smartest way to keep that `LINUX_DATA` drive current without wasting time.



---

### The 3-2-1 Strategy

Since you're ready to invest more in this, the industry standard is the **3-2-1 Rule**:

1. **3** copies of your data (Original + 2 backups).
2. **2** different types of media (e.g., your internal SSD and that external `LINUX_DATA` drive).
3. **1** copy off-site (like a cloud drive or a secondary drive at a friend's house) to protect against physical theft or fire.
